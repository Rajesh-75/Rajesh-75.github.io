The Full Encoder–Decoder Transformer

The original 2017 architecture from "Attention Is All You Need" — combining a bidirectional Encoder stack with an autoregressive Decoder stack, bridged by cross-attention. The architecture that replaced RNN Seq2Seq for machine translation and every Seq2Seq task since.

1 Why a Full Encoder–Decoder?

In earlier sessions we studied the RNN Encoder–Decoder architecture for machine translation. The Encoder read the source sentence and compressed it into a single context vector. The Decoder received that vector and generated the translation word by word. The fatal weakness was the context vector bottleneck — all source information had to fit into one fixed-size vector, which degraded badly for long sentences.

Bahdanau Attention (2015) partially fixed this by letting the Decoder attend to all Encoder hidden states at each decoding step. But the Encoder was still a sequential LSTM, processing one token at a time.

The full Encoder–Decoder Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) solved both problems at once:

The two problems solved simultaneously
  • Sequential bottleneck removed: the Encoder now processes all source tokens in parallel using Self-Attention — no more one-word-at-a-time RNN.
  • Context vector bottleneck removed: the Decoder attends directly to all Encoder output vectors at every generation step via Cross-Attention — no more single fixed-size summary vector.

2 The Encoder Stack

The Encoder in the full Transformer is identical to the Encoder-only architecture (BERT-style) you studied in the previous tutorial. It reads the full source sentence in parallel, using bidirectional Self-Attention so every token can attend to every other token simultaneously.

After passing through N identical Encoder layers, the output is a set of contextual vectors — one per source token — that carry rich, bidirectional understanding of the source sentence. These vectors are passed to every Decoder layer via Cross-Attention.

Figure 1: Encoder stack of the full Transformer. All 6 source tokens are processed in parallel. The 6 output vectors feed into every Decoder layer as the Keys and Values in cross-attention.

3 Cross-Attention — The Bridge

Cross-Attention is the most important new concept in the full Encoder–Decoder Transformer. It is the mechanism that connects the two stacks — allowing the Decoder to look directly at the Encoder's output at every generation step. This is what eliminates the context vector bottleneck entirely.

How cross-attention differs from self-attention

The Q, K, V sources are different
In self-attention, Q, K, and V all come from the same sequence (the Decoder's own tokens attend to each other).

In cross-attention, the split is:
  • Q (Query) — comes from the Decoder's current hidden state: "what am I looking for from the source?"
  • K (Key) — comes from the Encoder's output vectors: "what does each source token contain?"
  • V (Value) — comes from the Encoder's output vectors: "what information does each source token provide?"
$$\text{CrossAttention}(Q_\text{dec},\; K_\text{enc},\; V_\text{enc}) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_\text{dec} \cdot K_\text{enc}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \cdot V_\text{enc}$$

Figure 2: Cross-attention when generating "chat" (step 2). The Decoder Query asks "what source token is most relevant now?" The attention weight α = 0.72 on "cat" correctly identifies the alignment. Line thickness encodes attention weight.

Cross-attention vs Bahdanau Attention

This is conceptually the same idea as Bahdanau Attention — the Decoder looks at all Encoder hidden states and computes alignment scores. The key differences are:

Score function Additive MLP: v · tanh(W₁s + W₂h) Scaled dot-product: QKᵀ / √dₖ
Encoder backbone Sequential LSTM Parallel Self-Attention
Multi-head No — single attention Yes — H heads in parallel
Parallelisable No — RNN is sequential Yes — fully parallel
Q, K, V explicit? No — implicit in the MLP Yes — three explicit linear projections

4 The Decoder Stack

The Decoder stack is the most complex component — each layer contains three sublayers instead of two. The extra sublayer is the Cross-Attention that connects to the Encoder.

Three sublayers per Decoder layer
  1. Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention — the Decoder tokens attend to each other, but only to past positions (causal mask). Prevents peeking at future target tokens during training.
  2. Multi-Head Cross-Attention — the new sublayer. Decoder Queries attend to all Encoder output vectors (Keys and Values). This is the direct link to the source sentence.
  3. Feed-Forward Network — same as in the Encoder. Applied independently to each position. Followed by residual connection and layer norm.

Every sublayer is followed by a residual connection and layer normalisation.

Figure 3: Decoder stack of the full Transformer. Each layer has three sublayers: masked self-attention, cross-attention (marked ★), and feed-forward. The cross-attention bridge (amber, dashed arrow from left) receives K and V from the Encoder at every layer.

Teacher forcing during training

During training, the Decoder does not wait for its own previous prediction before generating the next token. Instead, the correct target tokens are fed in at every position simultaneously — this is called teacher forcing. The causal mask ensures the Decoder at position t still cannot see positions t+1 onwards. At inference time, the model switches to truly autoregressive generation — each predicted token is fed back as the next input.

6 Full Translation Walk-Through

Tracing exactly how "The cat sat on the mat" becomes "Le chat était sur le tapis":

Encoding phase — runs once

  1. Tokenise source: ["The", "cat", "sat", "on", "the", "mat"]
  2. Embed all 6 tokens and add positional encodings simultaneously.
  3. Pass all 6 token vectors through all N Encoder layers in parallel.
  4. Output: 6 contextual vectors E₁ … E₆ — one per source token. These are held fixed and reused at every decoding step.

Decoding phase — runs token by token

Step 1 — predicting "Le"
  • Input to Decoder: <s> (start token)
  • Masked self-attention: only one token, so attends to itself
  • Cross-attention: <s> query attends to E₁…E₆ — highest weight on "The" (sentence start)
  • Output: predict "Le"
Step 2 — predicting "chat"
  • Input to Decoder: <s>, "Le"
  • Masked self-attention: "Le" attends to <s> and itself
  • Cross-attention: "Le" query attends to E₁…E₆ — highest weight on "cat" (α = 0.72)
  • Output: predict "chat"
Step 3 — predicting "était"
  • Input to Decoder: <s>, "Le", "chat"
  • Masked self-attention: "chat" attends to all prior Decoder tokens
  • Cross-attention: "chat" query attends to E₁…E₆ — highest weight on "sat" (α ≈ 0.65)
  • Output: predict "était"

This continues until the Decoder predicts </s>. Notice that at every step the Decoder has direct access to all 6 Encoder vectors — there is no dilution over decoding steps. This is the decisive advantage over the RNN Encoder–Decoder you studied earlier.

Compare directly to RNN Encoder–Decoder
In the RNN version, generating "était" at step 3 meant relying on the context vector c that was 2 hidden state transitions away from its original source — the signal from "sat" had been diluted through s₁ and s₂. In the Transformer, "était" attends directly to E₃ (the "sat" Encoder vector) with no dilution at all.

7 The Equations in One Place

Encoder (per layer)

$$\text{EncAttnOut} = \text{LayerNorm}\!\left(x + \text{MultiHeadSelfAttn}(x, x, x)\right)$$ $$\text{EncOut} = \text{LayerNorm}\!\left(\text{EncAttnOut} + \text{FFN}(\text{EncAttnOut})\right)$$

Decoder (per layer — three sublayers)

$$\text{MaskAttnOut} = \text{LayerNorm}\!\left(y + \text{MaskedMultiHeadAttn}(y, y, y)\right)$$ $$\text{CrossAttnOut} = \text{LayerNorm}\!\left(\text{MaskAttnOut} + \text{MultiHeadAttn}(\underbrace{Q=\text{MaskAttnOut}}_{\text{from Decoder}},\; \underbrace{K=\text{EncOut},\; V=\text{EncOut}}_{\text{from Encoder}})\right)$$ $$\text{DecOut} = \text{LayerNorm}\!\left(\text{CrossAttnOut} + \text{FFN}(\text{CrossAttnOut})\right)$$

Output prediction

$$\hat{y}_t = \arg\max\;\text{softmax}\!\left(W_o \cdot \text{DecOut}_t + b_o\right)$$

where y is the target sequence (shifted right), x is the source sequence, and EncOut is fixed across all decoding steps.
