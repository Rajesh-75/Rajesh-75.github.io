From Statistical Foundations to Modern AI

A teaching portal for Masters and PhD students, researchers, and industry practitioners — built on decades of statistics teaching by Prof L.R.Potti(Yamuna Publications and VidyaPeetam) extended into today's ML, DL, and LLM systems.

The same foundation, carried forward — from probability and inference to modern neural networks

Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and Large Language Models have moved from specialized research topics to a foundational layer of modern knowledge work. They now shape how we search, write, diagnose, forecast, and build software, and an understanding of their underlying mathematics and algorithms is increasingly essential for any student or practitioner in engineering, science, or data-driven decision-making. Yet much of the available material either stays at a superficial, tool-usage level or jumps straight into unexplained code. This portal is built to close that gap.

The motivation for this portal comes from a specific place. My father, L. R. Potti, spent decades teaching and writing on Statistics and Operations Research, and his books stayed in demand for close to three decades because he built every idea — from a simple measure of dispersion to a full hypothesis test — on a rigorous, carefully reasoned foundation rather than a shortcut formula. This tutorial series is built out of that same body of teaching. It starts from that statistical foundation, and only then climbs into modern Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and LLM training — because the objectives, training procedures, and behaviour of every one of those modern systems reduce, at their core, to problems of estimation and inference that Statistics has always been about. The architectures and engineering that let them scale — convolution, attention, backpropagation, GPU-parallelized computation — draw on computer science and numerical optimization as well, but it is the statistical foundation that explains why an objective makes sense and why learning from data works at all. Studying ML and DL this way, grounded in the statistics first, is what makes the mathematics of a Transformer or a Neural Network feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.

This site is therefore a comprehensive, self-contained tutorial series that walks through classical Machine Learning, modern Deep Learning architectures, and the engineering of Large Language Models — each topic developed with the underlying mathematics explained in depth, the training procedure laid out as pseudo-algorithms, and worked examples on real-world data so that theory and practice reinforce each other. The series begins with the end-to-end Machine Learning pipeline, moves through the core classical algorithms and their hyperparameters, builds up to CNNs, RNNs, and Transformers (including a dedicated treatment of Encoder–Decoder architectures), and is now being extended into a page on building an LLM Engine. Underpinning all of it is a full Statistics track covering the subject the way it was taught to me — the same foundation that every algorithm elsewhere on this site rests on.

Statistics — In Honour of L. R. Potti

This Statistics track is dedicated to my father, L. R. Potti, a prolific author of Statistics and Operations Research books whose work was in demand across the field for nearly three decades. It follows the same syllabus he taught and wrote about for years, and is offered here in the hope of introducing his approach to a new generation of students — and of pointing readers toward his original books.

The topics below cover the full arc of classical statistics that underlies every algorithm elsewhere on this site: from summarizing data, to probability theory, to the inferential machinery of hypothesis testing and estimation.

Measures of Central Tendency Mean, median, mode, and how to choose between them. Measures of Dispersion Range, variance, standard deviation, and coefficient of variation. Skewness and Kurtosis Measuring asymmetry and tail behaviour in a distribution. Probability Theory Axioms, conditional probability, and independence. Probability Distributions Discrete and continuous distributions and where each one applies. Random Variables, Expectation & Variance Formalizing random variables and their moments. Bayesian Theory Prior, likelihood, and posterior — the Bayesian view of inference. Testing of Hypotheses Null and alternative hypotheses, test statistics, and significance. Estimation Theory Point and interval estimation, and properties of good estimators.
